# RC3.8 stakeholder release summary

## What changed?

The engine now turns its technical capabilities into one coherent product surface. It publishes a reproducible 12-scenario operational training pack, a plain-language capability map, a transparent treatment-admission status, a source-conformance scorecard, and privacy-bounded simulation telemetry. The release also shortens Windows test workspaces so the same assurance suite can run without path-length failures.

## What can a learner do now?

A learner can browse twelve fictional healthcare simulation scenarios grouped into four meaningfully different team challenges: direct handoff, communications relay, resource coordination, and a combined relay-plus-resource challenge. The learner can see what the scenario is meant to exercise, what evidence makes it reproducible, and which safety and authority limits apply.

## What can an instructor do now?

An instructor can select scenarios by operational challenge rather than by cosmetic wording alone. The instructor can also review a multidimensional source-conformance scorecard, see whether the evidence is sufficient for each dimension, inspect the hard safety gate, and understand why a treatment concept is blocked or still awaiting review.

## What changed in scenario variety?

The engine no longer counts a new name, location, weather description, or random seed as proof of a genuinely new behavior. It verifies four distinct operational policy classes and selects three reproducible scenarios from each class for an initial twelve-scenario pack. Human-team behavior is still structural and has not yet been empirically calibrated.

## What changed in scoring?

The engine now produces a transparent scorecard for situation assessment, information management, prioritization, communication, resource coordination, reassessment, closed-loop handoff, and safety. A critical safety failure cannot be averaged away. An incomplete run returns insufficient evidence instead of a fabricated percentage. The scorecard is source conformance only and is not a validated proficiency measure.

## What changed in treatment content?

Treatment concepts now have an explicit admission ladder. A concept must pass source-byte verification, evidence-span binding, applicability review, contraindication review, role and scope binding, simulated-effect adjudication, and independent attestation before it can become an active simulation choice. At this release, no concrete treatment is simulation-admitted.

## What safety limitation remains?

This release is for healthcare simulation and training. Direct patient care remains prohibited, clinical decision support remains prohibited, and the engine has no patient-care authority. A passing software test does not establish clinical correctness or permission to use the system on a real patient.

## What is still not calibrated or validated?

Operational timing remains not calibrated. Human-team behavior has not been empirically calibrated, patient dynamics remain source-bound static observations, and scoring has not been psychometrically validated. The telemetry and promotion controls added here are the foundation for later timing, scoring, and multisite validation work; they are not a claim that those scientific phases are already complete.
